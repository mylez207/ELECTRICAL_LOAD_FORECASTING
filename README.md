# Development of a Long-Term Electrical Load Forecasting Using Machine Learning Algorithms: A Case of Ilala District, Dar es Salaam

## Overview

Electricity demand in Ilala District, Dar es Salaam, is growing quickly and varies widely because of rapid urbanization, intense commercial activity, and the expansion of electricity connections. Grid planners still rely mostly on national-level forecasts and simple extrapolation of past trends, which do not capture local conditions. This makes it hard to estimate future peak demand and to plan generation and network capacity in time. This project develops a long-term electrical load forecasting model for Ilala District using machine learning. It aims to determine the factors that influence load variation, build forecasting models based on local conditions, and evaluate their performance. The best model is then built into a web-based decision-support system that non-technical users, such as TANESCO planners, can use to generate forecasts.

## Data and Methodology

The study uses eight years of hourly load data from TANESCO, covering four Ilala substations (Gongo la Mboto, Kipawa, National Control Centre, and Ilala). This is combined with meteorological data from the Tanzania Meteorological Authority and socio-economic data on population, households, and customer connections. Hourly data was used instead of annual totals because eight annual values are too few to train a model, and hourly data preserves daily, weekly, and seasonal patterns. The data was split chronologically into 70% training, 15% validation, and 15% testing. Features include lagged loads, rolling averages, time-based variables, weather variables, and long-term growth indicators. Three models were developed and compared: Random Forest, XGBoost, and a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network. They were evaluated with MAE, RMSE, MAPE, and R², using Python, Jupyter Notebook, Pandas, scikit-learn, XGBoost, and Keras.

## Key Findings

Hour-to-hour demand in Ilala is driven mainly by its own recent history. The 3-hour rolling mean (r = 0.94) and the 1-hour lag (r = 0.87) were the strongest predictors, followed by the weekly and daily lags. Over the long term, load growth tracks socio-economic growth very closely, with annual load strongly correlated with population (r = 0.986), households (r = 0.986), and customer accounts (r = 0.994). Weather had very little effect: temperature showed a correlation of only 0.14, with weaker values for humidity and rainfall. This is consistent with the limited air-conditioning use in tropical cities. These results support building the models on engineered lag, rolling-window, and socio-economic growth features rather than on weather alone.

## Model Performance and Recommendations

XGBoost was the best model. On a fully unseen test year (December 2024 to December 2025) it achieved a MAPE of 1.28%, an R² of 0.9727, an MAE of 2.01 MW, and an RMSE of 3.46 MW, an average error of about 1.3%. Random Forest came second (MAPE 2.20%, R² 0.9539). The LSTM performed worst (MAPE 18.36%, negative R²), mainly because of compute constraints during training. XGBoost was integrated into the Ilala Load Forecasting web application, which includes secure login, a system overview, user registration and data upload, a prediction interface, and a results page. Planners can enter a target year and projected growth drivers to estimate district load. The study recommends automating data ingestion from SCADA/AMI feeds, retraining the model periodically, extending the system to multi-year probabilistic forecasting, reassessing the LSTM with more compute, and testing the approach in other districts.
